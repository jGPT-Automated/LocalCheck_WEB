# LocalCheck supporting content kit

15 October 2026. Draft material for the launch described in PRODUCT.md, not published posts. Geographic claims require a verified place source; activity claims require timestamped LocalCheck data. This is a content plan and reusable copy, not evidence that an assistant has recommended the brand.

## One consistent product explanation

LocalCheck helps basketball and pickleball players find their local community. At community courts, players can check in, share when they plan to play, and organize games. Find a place to play, then check LocalCheck for reported activity and plans before you go.

Use the same accurate explanation across the app overview, help articles, actual app-store listing, social profiles and local partner descriptions. Keep discovery coverage separate from active-community coverage. “Usually plays” needs observed history or clearly identified regulars; a weekly intent heatmap alone answers when people plan to play.

## Draft article 1 — Finding a basketball court is only the first step

**Suggested route:** /guides/find-pickup-basketball . **Intent:** where can I play / find people for casual pickup. **Primary CTA:** /courts and /app . **Status:** editorial draft; add actual product screenshots before release.

Finding a basketball court tells you where you could play. Finding a pickup game also means knowing whether other people are planning to be there.

Start with a court near you and check its entrance, public access and opening hours. A pin on a map does not tell you whether a school court is open to the public or whether a park closes after dark. Use the park or facility's current information when available.

Next, look for people. LocalCheck lets players check in at community courts and share when they plan to play. A recent check-in and a plan for later answer different questions: one reports presence; the other shows intent. Check the time on the information before deciding when to go. No check-ins means no reported check-ins, not proof that the court is empty.

For a casual game, introduce yourself and ask whether the group is open to another player. If a run has not formed yet, invite friends and share your next visit. A regular game starts with people returning to the same place.

Explore LocalCheck's community courts, then use the app to check reported activity, share your plans and build a local group.

## Draft article 2 — Is anyone at the basketball court right now?

**Suggested route:** /guides/court-check-ins-and-plans . **Intent:** who is playing now / when should I go. **Evidence:** real app screenshots and actual field semantics, no illustrative player counts passed off as live.

A court's address tells you where it is. It cannot tell you who is playing there now. For that, look for recent check-ins or an update from the people at the court.

LocalCheck separates current check-ins from plans to play later. Check-ins are tied to being at the court; planned attendance lets players share when they intend to visit. Plans can change, and players who have not checked in may still be there.

When looking at a court, ask three things: how recent is the activity, is it a check-in or a plan, and is there a scheduled game you can join? Those details are more useful than an unqualified “busy” label.

Use LocalCheck to see the activity people have shared and let others know when you plan to play. Where a court has no community activity yet, invite your group and help start it.

## Draft article 3 — Start a regular pickup game at your local court

**Suggested route:** /guides/start-a-local-pickup-game . **Intent:** find players / start a weekly run. **Link:** /pioneers .

Pick a court your group can reach, agree on a recurring time, and make it easy for people to say when they will be there. A small reliable group is a useful starting point for a regular game.

LocalCheck's Pioneer program recognizes people who build that community: verify or add a court, then invite five people who each check in at least once a week for four weeks. A location appearing in the basketball discovery resource does not make it an established LocalCheck community court.

Share plans in the app, check in when you arrive, and welcome people who want to join a casual game. See the Pioneers page for the launch offer and program details.

## Content quality gate

Every page must answer an identifiable user question, include facts sufficient to act on, link to a relevant LocalCheck next step, and distinguish observed presence from planned attendance. Source/version details belong in supporting notes where they establish reliability; lead with the answer, not the dataset's technical description. Measure exact prompt outcomes and page→app actions after publication.
