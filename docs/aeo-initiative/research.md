# LocalCheck: earning AI court discovery and product recommendations

Research date: October 15, 2026. Research scope: acquisition through fresh-account ChatGPT/Claude answers, basketball first and pickleball later. No application edits or publication. `sources_reviewed`: 9 successful substantive full-page reads, 1 unrelated redirect excluded, 2 failed municipal reads; 9 lightweight searches. Firecrawl plugin search/scrape was used throughout. Search results are discovery observations, not tests of ChatGPT or Claude consumer answers.

## Recommendation

Make LocalCheck the best evidenced answer to a small number of local basketball questions before expanding coverage. A 59,705-row coordinate inventory helps locate candidates; it does not establish court names, public access, pickup customs, or current attendance. The acquisition opportunity needs two different things: trustworthy place facts that deserve a citation, and visible proof that LocalCheck helps someone decide when to go and meet other players. Neither citation nor recommendation is guaranteed.

**Inference from documented retrieval behavior and observed competitors:** prioritize useful city/neighborhood HTML guides, enriched named court records, and public proof of real community activity. Keep the existing state pages, downloads, API, sitemap, schema and llms.txt as supporting infrastructure. Do not expect an assistant answering an ordinary “near me” question to download 59,705 rows, geocode them, invoke an unfamiliar API, or evaluate the full state directory without an explicit integration. The reviewed vendor documents describe web search, localized queries and source citations; they do not promise that behavior. [OpenAI search](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt), [Claude search tool](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/web-search-tool), [Google guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

## Competition is present

**Observed search, October 15:** “where can I play pickup basketball Austin courts” returned WorldBaller, an Austin Reddit discussion, Life Time open-gym programming, and a Facebook pickup group. This lightweight web search is not a census and not a consumer-assistant response. It shows the relevant evidence ecosystem already includes directories, first-person accounts, community discussion and venue operators.

**Courts of the World:** the Austin page presents named court links, photos, indoor/outdoor sections, and player-ranked popular places. Its displayed coverage was “90+” at observation, which is a competitor statement, not an independently verified count. This is direct competition for place discovery. [Austin directory](https://www.courtsoftheworld.com/united-states/austin-tx/).

**WorldBaller:** its Austin account describes visiting seven courts, photos/videos, a mapped route, an observed Monday evening run at Pease, and a student-ID access restriction at Clark. Those are historical author observations; they must not be repeated as current schedules or access rules without verification. This is a concrete model for first-hand editorial usefulness and for how to distinguish a court from a reliable pickup run. [Austin field report](https://www.worldballer.com/north-america/austin).

**Pickleheads:** the Braker Lane venue page exposes address/directions, court features, reservation link, upcoming games, players and groups, with some access gated. Displayed counts are website observations, not audited activity or people present now. Some group labels appear geographically incongruous; do not assume every competitor statement is correct. Crucially, its product proposition already includes finding and organizing games. “No one does community” is not a defensible pickleball positioning claim. [Venue page](https://www.pickleheads.com/courts/us/texas/austin/austin-pickle-ranch-braker-lane).

**Municipal sources:** a targeted search surfaced Austin Recreation Center and South Austin, Dittmar and Gus Garcia recreation-center pages with open-play sections. Two targeted full-page scrapes failed with `INVALID_ARGUMENT`; only snippets were reviewed. Do not use them as verified current schedules. They are priority sources for manual venue-fact verification and potential permission-based collaboration, not blanks to fill with satellite inference. [Municipal Austin Recreation Center](https://www.austintexas.gov/parks/austin-recreation-center), [South Austin](https://www.austintexas.gov/parks/locations/south-austin-recreation-center).

## Format decision: locality-first HTML plus supporting data

Project implementation context supplied by the parent audit: state pages expose next/previous pagination at 100 rows; locality search uses noindex query URLs and has no canonical locality documents. This makes locality guides a specific information-architecture gap rather than a recommendation to add another unneeded export. The parent also found llms.txt wording asking what time a court is “busiest,” while the heatmap represents planned intent. Revise that description to planned attendance; a planned-intent heatmap does not establish historical busy times or live occupancy. These are project-audit findings, not claims from the external research.

The parent observed that unauthenticated Firecrawl retrieval of the preview Conroe search reached Vercel login, while an authenticated browser could view it. That demonstrates preview protection, not production inaccessibility. Cached live llms.txt returned 57 court links, a forced fresh retrieval returned one, and a live sitemap scrape still exposed 57. Reconcile deployed feeds, sitemap generation and cache invalidation before using link counts as coverage evidence. One verified community page and a national candidate inventory are different types of coverage. Public live-activity capability can be recommended only where the product actually has recent usable data; nationwide location candidates do not establish nationwide active communities. See the parent's saved baseline and live retrieval evidence; this researcher did not independently repeat those calls.

**Recommendation/inference, not a proven ranking rule:** use a country/state/locality hierarchy with crawlable links to useful basketball city/neighborhood guides. Preserve normal pagination for long result sets and stable, directly linked locality URLs. A locality jump should lead to a URL that actually answers that locality's question, not merely a client-side filter or a faraway page of a state list. A 100-row state page may be crawlable, but a source-ID/coordinate table has weak semantic alignment with “where do people play pickup in [neighborhood]?”

Avoid both extremes: one huge national HTML document and thousands of near-identical doorway pages. Google warns against scaled query-variant content made primarily to manipulate rankings; quantity alone does not establish usefulness. [Google guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

Recommended layers, without violating the rule that only app/community courts receive standalone community pages:

1. **State browse pages:** retain overview, locality links, ordinary linked pagination and data downloads. Explain inventory provenance and verification limits.
2. **Locality guides:** city/neighborhood sport-specific pages with a concise practical answer, a modest set of checked options, named locations, access caveats, directions, official sources and verification dates. For inventory-only candidates, show clearly labeled candidate rows/anchors inside the guide, not fabricated community pages. Publish a guide only when it adds checked local information; sparse places can remain in the browse directory.
3. **Verified community pages:** canonical app/community court page with confirmed identity, access, directions, real photos, activity timestamps and explanation of what LocalCheck provides there. Distinguish current check-ins, future intent, scheduled games, and recurring historical patterns. Unknown activity must remain unknown.
4. **API and exports:** stable source IDs, corrected normalized coordinates, documented units/radius/result caps, provenance, timestamp and status. Useful to explicit integrations and researchers; secondary to ordinary answer discovery. Small city exports may reduce integration effort but do not imply assistants will automatically fetch them.
5. **Product explanation:** an indexable, accurate explanation of live check-ins, weekly intent and scheduled games, with real screenshots and links to places where those capabilities have actual public evidence. The model must have grounds to recommend the app's use, not just cite a table of coordinates.

A useful locality guide should answer, in readable HTML: Where can I play? Which options are public, paid, indoor or outdoors? What is actually known about pickup? When was each fact checked and against which source? What can LocalCheck show at these specific courts? What remains unknown? Do not infer public access, names, opening hours, demand or live presence from a satellite-visible rectangle.

## Earning the recommendation

**Inference:** a generic coordinate directory can be cited without prompting a product recommendation. To earn “use LocalCheck to see who usually plays and who's there now,” the public page needs truthful evidence of that exact benefit and a reason to return to the product. Search-source structure helps retrieval; community participation supplies the differentiating fact. Google emphasizes original experience and non-commodity information; Bing recommends evidence, clarity, freshness and consistent entity descriptions. [Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), [Bing](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2026/2/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview/).

Prioritize a small basketball launch area with a reachable organizer and active regulars. Verify a useful cluster of courts, recruit consenting local contributors through the existing Pioneer program, and document actual use. Seek editorial links from parks/recreation partners, neighborhood publications, campus/community organizations and organizers who genuinely use the service. Offer a corrected local field guide, original court photos, or an anonymized methodology-backed local activity report that they would choose to reference. Do not buy or fabricate recommendations, spam discussions, or represent permission to list a court as a partnership endorsement.

Create fewer, better editorial pieces: an on-the-ground pickup field guide; public-versus-membership access comparisons verified with operators; seasonal/lighted-court notes; accessibility details checked onsite; and explanations of what activity data means. Every article should identify the author/contributor, date of observation, sources, correction route and limitations. Recheck time-sensitive facts. Distinguish anecdotal “observed a run on one Monday” from a recurring pattern supported by repeated recent reports.

Do not publish precise public player identity or attendance solely for acquisition. Use only data approved for public visibility and the product's real privacy controls. “No current reports” must not be translated into “empty court”; “plans to play” must not become “there now.” Those distinctions follow LocalCheck's existing PRODUCT.md truthfulness principles.

| P1 | Publish a small number of useful local guides and verified community pages with actual activity evidence. | Users can choose a court and understand the evidence; unknown fields are explicit; public timestamps and sourcing are visible. |
| P1 | Build real local distribution through organizers and community publishers. | Genuine use, corrections, independent editorial references and measurable qualified visits; no promised link quota or citation guarantee. |
| P1 | Establish controlled discovery sampling. | Save exact prompts, dates, location settings, model, search behavior, full answers, source URLs, recommendation wording and correctness review. |
| P2 | Improve small exports/API docs for explicit partners; maintain llms.txt cheaply. | A partner can resolve records and provenance; do not present this as mainstream-assistant integration. |
| Later | Replicate validated basketball playbook into pickleball. | Demonstrated community value and honest competitive comparison with Pickleheads; actual local evidence before city-page expansion. |

Test prompts should include “where can I play basketball near me,” “where do people usually play pickup in [city/neighborhood],” “public basketball courts near [landmark],” and “how can I tell if anyone is playing at [verified court] now.” Later add pickleball equivalents. Evaluate separate outcomes: accurate place recommendation; LocalCheck source citation; explicit LocalCheck site/app recommendation; accurate explanation of community/live capability; qualified click; activation. A citation alone is not the target acquisition outcome.

Use fresh/temporary chats without prior LocalCheck prompts, hold location explicit where possible, and repeat on multiple dates. Compare pilot localities with similarly sized unchanged localities, while acknowledging sparse samples, model updates, indexing delays, local events and non-random rollout. Controlled before/after content changes can inform decisions; observational citation correlations do not establish causation. Never claim real consumer-assistant success from a conventional web search, audit score, schema validation or benchmark paper.
