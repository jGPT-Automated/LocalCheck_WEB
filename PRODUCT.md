# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Local basketball and pickleball players deciding where and when to play, regulars organizing a run, competitive players logging reviewed results, and community builders putting missing courts on the map. Visitors arrive with practical questions: Is there a court near me, is anyone going, can I organize a game, and how does LocalCheck make competition trustworthy?

## Product Purpose

LocalCheck gives local communities one shared platform for their courts, activity, and competition. It replaces scattered group texts and uncertain court visits with a trustworthy picture of who is there now, who plans to play later, and what games are forming. The public website is the canonical source for verified courts. The mobile app adds live check-ins, weekly intent, scheduled games, reviewed results, friend, court, and regional Elo rankings, profiles, and verified court contribution. Success means a visitor can understand the full loop and see why the app is useful before they download or preview it.

## Brand Personality

Athletic, local, and truthful. The voice is direct and competitive without becoming macho or exclusionary. It should feel like floodlights coming on at a neighborhood court: specific, energized, and ready for play.

## Anti-references

Do not turn LocalCheck into a generic productivity SaaS page, a soft pastel wellness app, a purple-gradient tech landing page, or an endless grid of interchangeable feature cards. Do not fabricate player counts, ratings, testimonials, or court activity. Avoid stock UI, decorative glass, and feature copy that could belong to any sports app.

## Design Principles

1. Show the real product. App screens are evidence, not decoration.
2. Explain the hard parts. Give the heatmap, review flow, and Elo system enough space to become obvious.
3. Preserve trust. Clearly separate planned attendance, live presence, scheduled games, and finalized competition.
4. Tell one continuous story. Find a court, know when to go, show up, compete, settle the result, build the local scene.
5. Reward contribution, not vanity. Courts, players, and sustained community activity matter more than empty signups.

## Launch Programs

- Starters: the first 100 people to download and join LocalCheck receive one free year of LocalPlus. The website must not claim a live remaining-spots count without a verified entitlement feed.
- Pioneers: add a new court and invite five players who each check in at least once a week for four weeks. The Pioneer receives one free year of LocalPlus; each invited player receives one free month on sign-up.
- Website court spotlights come from the verified court feed: highest local count for basketball and pickleball, then live count and deterministic tie-breakers. Never simulate a check-in or player count in the UI.
- A website-wide global leaderboard may display only reviewed results for players eligible for public visibility. Until a privacy-safe public feed is available, show an honest empty state rather than sample names or scores.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and keyboard behavior. Respect reduced-motion preferences, never rely on color alone for heatmap intensity or match outcomes, maintain 44px minimum touch targets, and keep body copy readable at narrow widths.
